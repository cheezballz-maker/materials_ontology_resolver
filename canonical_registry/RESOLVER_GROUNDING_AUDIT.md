# Canonical Substance Resolver grounding audit

**Finding: remediation is required before relying on the lexical shortcuts for broader admission.** The identity-preserving architecture is defensible, but several active implementations are substantially narrower than the chemical principles they approximate. The sulfate/sulfite shortcut, case-folded lexical equivalence, duplicate-locant ownership inference, slash/colon component-ratio syntax, and the name-specific reviewed-evidence fixture deserve particular attention. This is an audit, not a finding that those rules caused a demonstrated false merge.

## Method and evidence boundaries

Audited the current production paths in `chemistry.py`, `form_characterizer.py`, `identity_comparator.py`, `policy.py`, `source_form.py`, `name_interpretation.py`, `systematic_notation.py`, `composition_constraints.py`, `evidence_retrieval.py`, `providers.py`, `resolver.py`, `registry_builder.py`, `pipeline.py`, `RegistryBridge.java`, `reviewed_evidence.json`, schema and the eight current test modules. Traced design and motivation through the initial form-aware work, exact-form retrieval, second holdout, source-form and source-name work, systematic naming, stereo propagation, OPSIN ownership investigation, salt feasibility and the [latest multicomponent report](EXPLICIT_MULTICOMPONENT_CONSTRAINT_UPGRADE_REPORT.md), including their referenced snapshots and prior CUMA validation. The molecular identity framework is the project contract, **not an independent nomenclature authority**.

The current 18 runtime artifacts and eight test files match the [latest frozen manifest](explicit_multicomponent_upgrade_release2/IMPLEMENTATION_FROZEN.json), as do the ontology and framework. The latest recorded run passed 129 tests; tests were not rerun or altered during this audit. The development replay reported 151 assessable correct EXACT admissions, one unverified admission and three assessable merged pairs. Those results, and project tests, are **implementation validation only**, not scientific grounding. No production code, tests, ontology, registry or outcomes were changed.

The inventory groups individual branches only where their evidential basis and risk are substantially alike. It covers active identity, parsing, source interpretation, retrieval, admission and clustering rules, plus documented historical policies. It is a desk audit, not a formal proof of every regex or third-party implementation. A source entry below identifies both what it supports and its limits; citations to that entry in the table do not imply that the source endorses the project's precise code or admission policy.

## Independent sources and what they establish

1. **IUPAC organic stereochemistry**, [Blue Book P-9](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P9.html), especially P-91 to P-93: R/S and E/Z describe configurations; cis/trans and syn/anti have context-specific relative uses; D/L is a class-specific convention. Configuration is distinct from conformation. It does **not** establish regex completeness, OPSIN atom ownership, or a universal source-specificity gate.
2. **IUPAC carbohydrate nomenclature**, [2-Carb-6](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/2carb/06n07.html) and [Blue Book P-10](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P10.html): anomeric and ring nomenclature, and equilibrium mixtures, are real distinctions. They do **not** say every name containing a selected root is a free monosaccharide or that every derivative requires an anomer token.
3. **IUPAC isotope nomenclature**, [P-82 and P-83](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P8.html): isotopic substitution, positional nuclides and labelled mixtures have distinct nomenclature. It does **not** validate this resolver's small isotope regex.
4. **IUPAC salts and components**, [Blue Book P-65.6](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P6.html), [P-77](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P7.html), and [inorganic brief guide, tables 1–4](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BriefGuide/inorganic.html): salt names, ionic charge, multiplicative prefixes and formal addition compositions are established. Prefix interpretation depends on grammar and entity scope. These sources do **not** justify inferring every proton-transfer graph, treating a single chloride depiction as all possible salt structures, or the resolver's fixed component vocabulary.
5. **IUPAC addition compounds and hydrate**, [Blue Book P-14.8](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P1.html), [Red Book IR-5.5](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/bibliog/RedBook2005.pdf), and [Gold Book “hydrate”](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/15195): in applicable addition-compound nomenclature fixed hydrate prefixes give composition; water may instead be involved in covalent hydration or coordination. The prescribed component delimiter is an em dash and ratios use solidi, not the project's arbitrary slash/colon grammar. Composition alone does **not** establish a unique disconnected molecular graph, lattice, polymorph, or water binding.
6. **IUPAC tautomers**, [Gold Book “tautomer”](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/12734): tautomers are structural isomers differing in hydrogen attachment and bond placement. It does **not** mandate that every registry distinguish all tautomers or validate any specific tautomer-enumeration algorithm.
7. **InChI Trust**, [technical manual, standard/nonstandard layers](https://www.inchi-trust.org/wp/download/104/InChI_TechMan.pdf), and [FAQ §§6.2, 15.12–15.14](https://www.inchi-trust.org/download/104/inchi-faq.pdf): Standard InChI's tautomer-invariant representation, proton/charge layers and disconnected-metal handling can lose distinctions; Fixed-H is a nonstandard option for particular tautomeric H placement. A full key is a condensed retrieval identifier. Neither key equality nor Fixed-H universally proves this project's exact graph identity or source intention; independent executables may share reference library code.
8. **FDA GSRS**, [registration guidance pp. 2–3](https://www.fda.gov/media/169513/download) and [field definitions, moieties](https://open.fda.gov/fields/othersubstance_reference.pdf): substance definitions include structures, stereochemical information and component ratios; moieties typically represent disconnected covalent structures. GSRS also supports broader substance types and does **not** require the project's narrow molecular-only scope or prove source/database independence.
9. **CAS naming and indexing**, [2007 guide §§103–104, 108](https://web.cas.org/marketing/pdf/indexguideapp.pdf): inverted index names and punctuation-sensitive names are established. The example is a substituted butanesulfonic acid/ester; it does **not** validate a generic inversion regex or arbitrary hyphen/space interchange.
10. **WHO INN**, [guidance: names for radicals and modified INNs](https://www.who.int/teams/health-product-and-policy-standards/inn/guidance-on-inn): names such as oxacillin sodium distinguish an active moiety and its salt. INN naming does **not** by itself specify every site of proton transfer or this registry's equality relation.
11. **Representation and tool documentation**: [OpenSMILES §§3.5–3.6](https://opensmiles.org/opensmiles.html) distinguishes atom/charge/isotope/bond syntax and equivalent H renderings; [RDKit SMILES writer](https://www.rdkit.org/new_docs/cppapi/SmilesWrite_8h_source.html) documents canonical and isomeric output; [RDKit Book](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/RDKit_Book.html) documents toolkit-specific aromaticity and stereo; [PubChem search help](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/search/help_search.html) says structure searches undergo PubChem standardization; [Unicode normalization FAQ](https://www.unicode.org/faq/normalization.html) defines NFC text equivalence. These establish formats and software behavior, **not** universal chemical identity or equivalence of punctuation, case, names, source claims or providers. The resolver's own `RegistryBridge.java` shows how it calls OPSIN/InChI; parser success is not authoritative nomenclature proof.

## Salt, hydrate and ratio reassessment

The [IUPAC inorganic guide](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BriefGuide/inorganic.html) gives independent examples of sodium(1+), numerical prefixes and magnesium sulfate heptahydrate; [P-65.6](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P6.html) explicitly gives **disodium carbonate**, i.e. two Na+ for a carbonate unit. [WHO's INN list](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/international-nonproprietary-names-%28inn%29/rl01.pdf?download=true) provides independently documented disodium salts, and [WHO's dehydroemetine dihydrochloride monograph](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/medicines/pharmacopoeia/omitted-monographs/dehydroemetine_dihydrochloride.pdf) states a 1:2 base/HCl formula. These examples justify investigating literal multiplicities independently of CUMA; they do not justify choosing a protonation-site graph for every acid-addition name. The present `singleton_count` graph check is an implementation choice and may not cover covalently represented metals, solids or formula-unit scaling.

The [IUPAC addition-compound convention](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P1.html) supplies independent organic examples **ethanol—pyridine (1/1)** and base—phosphoric acid **(1/2)**; [WHO's pharmacopoeial comparison](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/2023-eml-expert-committee/applications-for-addition-of-new-medicines/a33_phosphorus.pdf?sfvrsn=9f401afc_2), p. 14, independently lists sodium phosphate monohydrate and dihydrate. Their naming convention supports source-specific stoichiometry, not automatic disconnected-water bonding, crystal structure or a general `A / B (1:2)` parser. A separate [IARC monograph](https://publications.iarc.who.int/_publications/media/download/2958/04f67887be058cb7ae62b10572bf41e37643b15c.pdf), p. 148, shows a **CAS-style `hydrochloride (1:2)`**, demonstrating that colon ratios exist in a particular index style; it does not establish how to assign *arbitrary* colon ratios or slash-separated components. The current regex is a project-specific convention, not the cited IUPAC notation.

The Gold Book explicitly gives **chloral hydrate** as an example of water incorporated into a molecule. An unqualified `hydrate` need not identify a fixed number. Fractional names such as `hemihydrate` are established composition terminology; abstaining is the current molecular scope, not a claim that such materials are chemically invalid. A `form_kind=hydrate` assertion or separately typed graph is a source claim whose provenance must be trusted; it is not independent proof of hydration semantics. The older form characterizer can still require disconnected water based on a suffix before the new source constraint layer has established water-of-addition meaning. Its behavior should be independently reviewed, including false conflicts and false abstentions.

## Lexical exceptions and source ownership

The narrow ethanol inversion has a real background: CAS documents inverted index headings, and [PubChem CID 34](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/34) displays both `Ethanol, 2-chloro-` and `2-chloroethanol`. The CAS guide, not that one synonym hit, grounds *index inversion as a practice*. Neither validates the exact four-halogen/numeric-locant regex; its motivation and tests are restricted, so quarantine is warranted pending independent cases and source-specific grammar. A parser's agreeing output is implementation evidence, not nomenclature authority.

Sulfate and sulfite are real distinct chemical concepts. Comparing independently parsed, source-scoped complete structures would be more general than scanning every sulfur for three or four oxygen neighbors. That replacement must still abstain if either claim is unparseable or the names do not establish the conflicting local fragment. The current token-specific preferred/alias conflict also cannot establish that the two words refer to the same syntactic scope. The previous [source-form report](SOURCE_FORM_INTERPRETATION_UPGRADE_REPORT.md) identifies the CUMA 5684 failure as the motivation; independent chemistry grounds the distinction, **not this detector**.

The sugar anomer concern is independently real, with α/β-D-glucopyranose in [IUPAC carbohydrate recommendations](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/2carb/06n07.html). The current `gluc|galact|mann|fruct|rib|xyl|arab|idos|fucos` root scan, alditol suffix exception and decorated-ring proxy do not amount to a carbohydrate grammar. The 30142 glucose failure discovered a valid source-specificity problem, but does not validate the full vocabulary or apply anomer rules to all recognized derivatives.

The [stereo propagation report](STEREO_PROPAGATION_LOCANT_SCOPING_UPGRADE_REPORT.md) ties the duplicate-locant recovery to two CUMA records. A leading descriptor and one potentially stereogenic same-numbered source atom are useful evidence but are not a demonstrated rule of name-fragment ownership. The isolated [OPSIN ownership investigation](OPSIN_OWNERSHIP_TRACE_FOLLOWUP_REPORT.md) did not establish general ownership or clone lineage and was not deployed. Keep this distinction separate from the independently established meaning of R/S or E/Z.

Further independent non-CUMA examples include [IUPAC's isotopically substituted `(1R)-(1-²H₁)ethan-1-ol` and L-(4-¹³C,³⁵S)methionine](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P8.html), and the D/L and cis/trans examples in [P-9](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BlueBook/P9.html). They support nomenclature principles, **not** coverage of the current token regexes. No independent broad example suite for the duplicate-locant exception, alphabetic hyphen equivalence, sugar-root proxy, or slash/colon ratio convention was identified in this audit.

## Architecture assessment and minimal remediation

Separating a source concept from a canonical ID, search from resolution, and evidence retrieval from identity policy is sound registry engineering supported by [FDA's definitional-reference and structure model](https://www.fda.gov/media/169513/download), although FDA's broader registry granularity is different. Preserving source-relevant stereo, isotope, protonation, components and explicit form is chemically justified; deciding which forms get separate SUB IDs is this project's explicit policy. Source specificity bounding candidate specificity, aliases as claims, and abstention on insufficient evidence are defensible evidential principles rather than universal nomenclature mandates. Standard InChIKey alone is inadequate as exact-graph proof under the chosen form policy. Ordinary conformers are not automatically different identities, with supported exceptions such as isolable atropisomers requiring separate treatment. The current source-name parser and a single provider can provide correlated evidence; neither the architecture nor this audit proves their independence.

1. **Quarantine the admission-relevant duplicate-locant ownership shortcut, alphabetic hyphen/casefold source-equivalence shortcut, ungrounded ratio grammar and source-name-specific fixture** pending source-grammar and provenance review. Do not turn quarantine into a broader admission rule. The fixture can be retained as documented, timestamped case evidence outside generic runtime policy if its independent provenance is rechecked; it currently only vetoes.
2. **Consider removing the sulfate/sulfite-specific detectors** after replacing their safety role with source-scoped independently parsed structure comparisons; preserve abstention when scope or a parse is unknown. Review the sugar-root proxy and element/solvent allowlists for similar overreach without removing existing conservative outcomes blindly.
3. Reconcile the earlier hydrate and ionic-word shortcuts with the source-explicit constraint layer; preserve H placement, component multiplicity, existing graph checks and contradiction handling. Treat a generic or unsupported case as unresolved, not as a guessed structure.
4. Add independent, non-CUMA positive and negative nomenclature examples for each retained shortcut, including locally scoped duplicate locants, case-sensitive descriptors, index inversion, mixed anomer/ring names, fixed vs covalent/fractional hydrates, and source-specific component-ratio syntax. Separate authoritative semantics, tool behavior and implementation validation. Reassess lineage dependence rather than counting database brands.
5. Re-run frozen safety regressions and perform a genuinely fresh blinded evaluation only after a separately authorized remediation. No code changes or new resolver capabilities are proposed as part of this audit.
