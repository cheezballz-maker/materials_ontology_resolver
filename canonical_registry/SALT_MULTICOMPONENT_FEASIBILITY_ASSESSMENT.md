## Evidence and interpretation rules

Twenty cases were selected from frozen prior adjudications to cover ionic, acid-addition, explicit ratios, hydrated forms, stereochemical ambiguity, solids, coordination and mixtures. Nineteen are from the later 400-record development replay; dipotassium case 6978 is from the earlier blinded-validation snapshot and was checked with a **source-only** current OPSIN/form probe, not a full resolver rerun. These are deliberately varied examples, not a representative sample or a new validation set. Frozen references are evidence for review, not automatic overrides of the present identity contract. [Selected source claims, adjudication reasons, structures and reference URLs](salt_feasibility_audit/cases_raw.json).

A name can support a *composition constraint* without specifying a unique molecular graph. IUPAC distinguishes compositional names from structural information and permits numbered hydrate nomenclature; it also uses “hydrate” for more than isolated water of crystallization, including some covalently hydrated species. Thus a fixed prefix supplies a count only in the applicable water-of-addition sense; it cannot by itself establish a disconnected-water graph or a crystal structure. [IUPAC inorganic nomenclature](https://iupac.qmul.ac.uk/BriefGuide/inorganic.html), [IUPAC Gold Book: hydrate](https://goldbook.iupac.org/terms/view/15195).

In the table, **constraint only** means the source can inform future checks but not automatically create an exact identity; **candidate for narrow envelope** means further validated component and corroboration checks could support the indicated graph, not that this task admitted it. A coefficient on hydrochloride denotes an acid-addition composition claim; it is not a source-specified location for proton transfer. Existing frozen `EXACT` labels are retained as historical judgments, including neutral acid-adduct drawings, and do not override the present requirement to preserve explicitly supported protonation and charge.

The local form probe also rejects a two-water graph against a monohydrate name and holds an unnumbered hydrate. Its ethanol examples are **synthetic graph checks**, not claims that a particular ethanol hydrate exists. The InChI probes are likewise comparison diagnostics, not identity evidence for the selected CUMA names.

## Proposed safe boundary

A narrowly scoped future implementation could first extract **source constraints**, independently of retrieval: named component roles; explicit per-formula-unit integer ratios; source-established component identities, charges and hydrogen placement; and, when the source clearly denotes a water-of-addition hydrate, its fixed positive integer water count. These claims should remain separate from candidates. Accept a canonical molecular identity only when all of the following are established:

1. Each constituent is an in-scope, discrete molecular or ionic identity with its necessary constitution, stereo, isotope, tautomer, protonation and charge specified by the source or an explicit validated source structure.
2. Every constituent and positive integer coefficient is explicit and unambiguously attached to the correct formula unit. A binary salt's ratio may be derived by charge balance **only when** the source already establishes a complete neutral binary composition and each constituent's charge; do not guess missing counterions, charged species or H positions. For a first increment, requiring explicit ratios is the safer subset.
3. A validated candidate retains the same component graphs, formal charges, hydrogen placement and multiplicities; an isolated HCl dot component and a proton-transferred ionic pair are not silently substituted for one another. Source-derived OPSIN success or one provider result alone is insufficient.
4. All existing strict structure, identity-comparison, official InChI, source-form, corroboration and contradiction checks pass. Any conflicting graph, unknown relevant dimension or incomplete evidence remains CONFLICT or UNRESOLVED under the existing policy.

**Fixed hydrates can be included conditionally** as an already specified molecular/ionic formula unit plus exactly *n* discrete H2O components when water-of-addition semantics and stoichiometric attachment are established, the graph preserves them, and the identity is otherwise complete. Monohydrate, dihydrate and trihydrate convey 1, 2 and 3 waters per formula unit under that interpretation; no solid-state ontology or polymorph identity is required for this limited compositional claim. Unnumbered `hydrate`, `hydrated`, `xH2O`, variable hydration, uncertain placement of the coefficient, covalent hydration and coordinated water stay unresolved or out of scope. Fractional hemihydrates and extended solids remain outside the proposed first envelope.

Hold unspecified salt/counterion or ratios; mixed or variable composition; uncertain proton transfer, protonation site or zwitterion; parent-form inference; incomplete stereo/isotope/tautomer; coordination ambiguity; extended solids; and any candidate that adds or drops components not supported by source evidence. Source constraints may still be recorded for these cases without allocating a Substance ID.

## Recommended next work

A **smaller, source-independent constraint extractor and checker** for explicit component identities, integer counts, charges and fixed water-of-addition counts is justified for design and targeted testing. It should feed the existing form gate and compare preserved graphs, not expand the identity policy, infer proton transfers or add a parent-selection rule. The selected cases show why a broader one-step salt admission upgrade is premature: a component count can be clear while stereo, charge topology, evidence conflict, coordination or material scope remains unresolved. A separate fresh blinded evaluation is needed before any scaling decision.
